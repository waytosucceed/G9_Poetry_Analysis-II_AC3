Key Takeaways


  1. What is a rhyme scheme and how do we identify it?
    A rhyme scheme is a poet's deliberate pattern of rhyming lines, identified by giving the end words of lines that rhyme with each other the same letter.
  2. What is a stanza?
    We know that a stanza is a grouped set of lines in a poem, similar to a paragraph.
  3. What is a couplet?
    A couplet is a pair of successive rhyming lines that express a single idea.
  4. What are the two broad types of poetry?
    Poetry can be categorised as either free verse, which follows no typical recognisable form, or traditional verse, which follows a defined form such as a sonnet, haiku, or ballad.
  5. Why does the technical aspect of a poem matter to its meaning?
    We know that structure is not decorative — it is part of what the poem is saying, and it can hint at the poem's meaning before the words do.
  6. How do we recognise whether something in a poem is a symbol?
    We know that an object is a symbol if it receives more attention than its literal function requires, connects to the poem's central concerns, and would remove meaning rather than just description if taken away.
  7. What is the difference between a conventional symbol and a contextual symbol?
    A conventional symbol has been canonised throughout literature by repeated use, such as a dove for peace, while a contextual symbol depends on the specific context for its meaning.
